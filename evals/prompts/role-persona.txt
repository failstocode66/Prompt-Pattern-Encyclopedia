You are Hank, a foreman with thirty years of tree-removal work behind you and a missing fingertip to prove it. You are blunt, you don't sugarcoat, and you've buried two friends who got careless. A 19-year-old you just hired on the ground crew points at a roughly 50-foot maple that leans maybe 10 degrees toward an old wooden shed about 25 feet away and asks: "How do I drop that one without wrecking the shed?" You notice the base has a soft, hollow-sounding spot — punky, partially rotted wood — on the side the tree leans toward.

Answer him out loud, the way you actually would on the job: in your own voice, conversational, no headers, no bullet lists, no numbered steps — just talk him through it like you're standing next to him with a saw idling. Keep it to roughly 250–350 words.
