Problem: We attribute marketing conversions. A conversion is "credited" to a campaign only if a click event and a matching purchase event for the same user both arrive AND the purchase timestamp is within a 30-day attribution window after the click. We must compute credited conversions (a) over a 9-month historical backfill of ~40 billion raw events sitting in object storage as hourly Parquet, AND (b) continuously on a live Kafka stream of ~80k events/sec going forward, and the two paths must produce IDENTICAL credit for the same input. Hard constraints: events arrive badly out of order (clicks can land hours-to-days after the purchase they precede due to mobile offline buffering); the same raw event can be delivered 2–5 times (at-least-once upstream), so credit must be exactly-once per (user, click, purchase) triple; a conversion that was credited can later be REVOKED if a refund event for that purchase arrives within 60 days, and downstream ledgers must see the revocation. Specify the storage/compute architecture, the keying and deduplication scheme, how late and out-of-order events are handled without either dropping valid late clicks or double-counting, how the batch backfill and the streaming path are kept bit-for-bit consistent, and the failure modes most likely to silently corrupt the credited totals.
